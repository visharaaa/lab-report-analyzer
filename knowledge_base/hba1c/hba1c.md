# HbA1c

## What it is

HbA1c, also called hemoglobin A1c or glycated hemoglobin, is a form of hemoglobin that has glucose attached to it.

The HbA1c test provides an estimate of a person's average blood glucose level over approximately the previous two to three months.

## What the test measures

The HbA1c test measures the percentage of hemoglobin that has glucose attached to it.

Unlike a blood glucose test, HbA1c does not represent the glucose level at one specific moment.

HbA1c testing does not normally require fasting.

## Why it may be tested

HbA1c may be used to screen for or help diagnose prediabetes and type 2 diabetes.

It is also commonly used to monitor blood glucose control in people who have already been diagnosed with diabetes.

## When the result is low

A lower HbA1c generally indicates lower average blood glucose levels over the period represented by the test.

An unusually low or unexpected result may need additional context, particularly if the person has a condition that affects red blood cells or hemoglobin.

HbA1c should not be interpreted in isolation.

## When the result is high

A higher HbA1c generally indicates higher average blood glucose levels over the preceding two to three months.

Elevated HbA1c values can be associated with prediabetes or diabetes when the test is being used for diagnosis and the appropriate diagnostic criteria are met.

A single result should not automatically be treated as a diagnosis, particularly when the person does not have clear symptoms or when factors that can affect HbA1c are present.

## Important context

HbA1c is different from fasting, random, and postprandial blood glucose because it reflects longer-term average glucose rather than a single measurement.

Certain conditions that affect red blood cells or hemoglobin can make HbA1c less reliable. Examples include some forms of anemia, recent blood loss or transfusion, kidney disease, liver disease, and certain hemoglobin variants.

When HbA1c does not agree with other glucose measurements, additional testing or clinical evaluation may be appropriate.

Diagnostic thresholds are different from a laboratory's general reference range. The system should preserve the laboratory-provided reference range and should not treat an out-of-range result alone as a diagnosis.

## Safety note

An HbA1c result outside the provided laboratory reference range does not by itself establish a diagnosis.

This knowledge base provides general educational information. The system should not diagnose diabetes or prediabetes or recommend treatment based on an HbA1c result alone.
