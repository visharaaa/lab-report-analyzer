# Hemoglobin

## What it is

Hemoglobin is an iron-rich protein found in red blood cells. It carries oxygen from the lungs to tissues throughout the body.

## What the test measures

A hemoglobin test measures the amount of hemoglobin in the blood. It is commonly included as part of a complete blood count (CBC).

## Why it may be tested

A hemoglobin test may be used as part of a routine blood test, to investigate possible anemia, or to monitor conditions or treatments that can affect red blood cells and hemoglobin levels.

## When the result is low

A low hemoglobin result may be associated with conditions such as anemia, blood loss, iron deficiency, vitamin B12 or folate deficiency, chronic diseases, kidney disease, or conditions affecting red blood cell production or destruction.

A low result does not identify the underlying cause by itself. Other laboratory results and clinical information may be needed to determine the cause.

## When the result is high

A high hemoglobin result can have several possible causes. These may include dehydration, smoking, living at high altitude, prolonged low oxygen levels, certain heart or lung conditions, or conditions that cause increased red blood cell production.

A high result does not identify the underlying cause by itself.

## Important context

Hemoglobin results should be interpreted using the reference range provided by the laboratory. Reference ranges can vary between laboratories and may also vary depending on factors such as age, sex, pregnancy, and altitude.

The hemoglobin result should also be considered alongside other CBC results and relevant clinical information.

## Safety note

A laboratory result outside the provided reference range does not by itself establish a diagnosis.

This knowledge base provides general educational information. The system should not diagnose a medical condition or recommend treatment based on a hemoglobin result alone.
