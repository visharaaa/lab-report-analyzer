# WBC Count

## What it is

White blood cells (WBCs), also called leukocytes, are blood cells that are an important part of the immune system. They help the body respond to infections and other conditions.

There are five major types of white blood cells: neutrophils, lymphocytes, monocytes, eosinophils, and basophils.

## What the test measures

A WBC count measures the total number of white blood cells in the blood. It is commonly included as part of a complete blood count (CBC).

A separate blood differential test measures the different types of white blood cells.

## Why it may be tested

A WBC count may be used as part of a routine CBC or to help investigate or monitor conditions involving infection, inflammation, the immune system, or blood cell production.

## When the result is low

A low WBC count, called leukopenia, can be associated with conditions affecting the bone marrow or immune system. It may also occur with certain infections, autoimmune disorders, some cancers, or as a result of certain medicines or treatments.

A low WBC count alone does not identify the underlying cause.

## When the result is high

A high WBC count, called leukocytosis, can occur with infections, inflammation, allergic reactions, tissue injury, physical or emotional stress, smoking, pregnancy, or certain medicines.

Some blood disorders and cancers can also be associated with high WBC counts.

A high WBC count alone does not identify the underlying cause.

## Important context

The WBC count should be interpreted together with the patient's other CBC results and, when available, the blood differential.

The proportion and number of individual white blood cell types can provide additional information because neutrophils, lymphocytes, monocytes, eosinophils, and basophils have different roles.

Reference ranges can vary between laboratories. The reference range printed on the patient's laboratory report should be used when determining whether the result is low, normal, or high.

## Safety note

A WBC result outside the provided reference range does not by itself establish a diagnosis.

This knowledge base provides general educational information. The system should not diagnose an infection, immune disorder, or other medical condition based on a WBC count alone.
