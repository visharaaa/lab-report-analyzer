# Neutrophils

## What it is

Neutrophils are a type of white blood cell and an important part of the body's immune system. They help defend the body against infections and respond to inflammation.

## What the test measures

A neutrophil result indicates the amount or proportion of neutrophils in the blood.

In a blood differential, neutrophils may be reported as a percentage of total white blood cells or as an absolute neutrophil count (ANC).

## Why it may be tested

Neutrophils may be measured as part of a CBC with differential to help evaluate infections, inflammation, immune system activity, or conditions affecting blood cell production.

## When the result is low

A low neutrophil result is called neutropenia when the absolute neutrophil count is below the relevant reference threshold.

Neutropenia can occur with certain infections, autoimmune conditions, nutritional deficiencies, bone marrow disorders, or some medicines and medical treatments.

The significance of a low result depends partly on the absolute neutrophil count and the individual's clinical context.

## When the result is high

A high neutrophil result, called neutrophilia, can occur when the body is responding to infection or inflammation.

It can also occur in response to physical stress, injury, smoking, or certain medicines.

A high neutrophil result alone does not identify the underlying cause.

## Important context

Neutrophils can be reported as either a percentage or an absolute count. These measurements should not be treated as interchangeable.

The percentage of neutrophils can change because the proportions of other white blood cell types change, even when the absolute neutrophil count remains within its reference range.

Neutrophils should therefore be interpreted alongside the total WBC count and other differential results.

Reference ranges vary between laboratories and may depend on the measurement method.

The reference range printed on the patient's laboratory report should be used when determining whether the result is low, normal, or high.

## Safety note

An abnormal neutrophil result does not by itself establish an infection or another diagnosis.

This knowledge base provides general educational information. The system should not diagnose an infection or immune disorder based on a neutrophil result alone.
