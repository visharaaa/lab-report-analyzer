# RBC Count

## What it is

Red blood cells (RBCs), also called erythrocytes, are blood cells that contain hemoglobin and carry oxygen from the lungs to tissues throughout the body.

## What the test measures

An RBC count measures the number of red blood cells in a person's blood. It is usually included as part of a complete blood count (CBC).

## Why it may be tested

An RBC count may be performed as part of a routine CBC, to investigate possible blood disorders such as anemia, or to monitor conditions or treatments that may affect red blood cell levels.

## When the result is low

A low RBC count can be associated with conditions such as anemia, blood loss, nutritional deficiencies, kidney disease, problems affecting blood cell production, or certain chronic diseases.

A low RBC count alone does not identify the underlying cause. Other blood tests and clinical information may be needed for interpretation.

## When the result is high

A high RBC count can have several possible causes, including dehydration, living at high altitude, smoking, certain heart or lung conditions, sleep apnea, or conditions that cause increased red blood cell production.

A high RBC count alone does not identify the underlying cause.

## Important context

RBC reference ranges can vary between laboratories and may depend on factors such as age, sex, altitude, and other characteristics.

RBC count should be interpreted together with other CBC measurements, including hemoglobin, hematocrit, and red blood cell indices such as MCV, MCH, MCHC, and RDW.

The reference range printed on the patient's laboratory report should be used when determining whether the result is low, normal, or high.

## Safety note

A laboratory result outside the provided reference range does not by itself establish a diagnosis.

This knowledge base provides general educational information. The system should not diagnose a medical condition or recommend treatment based on an RBC count alone.
