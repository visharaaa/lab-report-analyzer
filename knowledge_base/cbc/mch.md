# MCH

## What it is

MCH stands for mean corpuscular hemoglobin. It represents the average amount of hemoglobin contained in an individual red blood cell.

## What the test measures

An MCH measurement indicates the average amount of hemoglobin in each red blood cell. It is one of the red blood cell indices reported as part of a complete blood count (CBC).

## Why it may be tested

MCH is used together with other CBC measurements to evaluate red blood cells and help characterize different types of anemia.

## When the result is low

A low MCH means that red blood cells contain less hemoglobin than expected based on the laboratory's reference range.

A low MCH can occur with iron deficiency and some other conditions affecting hemoglobin production.

A low MCH alone does not identify the underlying cause.

## When the result is high

A high MCH means that red blood cells contain more hemoglobin on average than expected based on the laboratory's reference range.

A high MCH can occur when red blood cells are larger than usual, such as in some types of anemia. Other causes are possible.

A high MCH alone does not identify the underlying cause.

## Important context

MCH should be interpreted alongside other red blood cell measurements, particularly MCV, MCHC, RDW, hemoglobin, hematocrit, and RBC count.

MCH describes the average amount of hemoglobin per red blood cell. It should not be interpreted as a direct measurement of the body's total iron stores.

Reference ranges can vary between laboratories.

The reference range printed on the patient's laboratory report should be used when determining whether the result is low, normal, or high.

## Safety note

An MCH result outside the provided reference range does not by itself establish a diagnosis.

This knowledge base provides general educational information. The system should not diagnose anemia or another medical condition based on MCH alone.
