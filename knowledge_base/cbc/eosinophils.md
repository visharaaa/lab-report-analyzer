# Eosinophils

## What it is

Eosinophils are a type of white blood cell involved in the body's immune system. They can participate in immune responses associated with allergies, inflammation, and certain infections.

## What the test measures

An eosinophil result indicates the amount or proportion of eosinophils in the blood.

In a blood differential, eosinophils may be reported as a percentage of total white blood cells or as an absolute eosinophil count.

## Why it may be tested

Eosinophils may be measured as part of a CBC with differential to help investigate allergic conditions, certain infections, inflammation, and other conditions that can affect eosinophil levels.

## When the result is low

Low eosinophil levels are generally not considered significant on their own and may occur because eosinophil levels are normally very small.

Certain medicines and conditions that affect hormone levels can also influence eosinophil levels.

A low result alone does not identify an underlying condition.

## When the result is high

A high eosinophil count, called eosinophilia, can occur with allergic conditions, certain parasitic infections, some skin conditions, and other inflammatory or immune-related conditions.

Some medicines and certain blood disorders can also be associated with increased eosinophil levels.

A high result alone does not identify the underlying cause.

## Important context

Eosinophils may be reported as a percentage or as an absolute count. These measurements should not be treated as interchangeable.

The absolute eosinophil count can provide additional information when evaluating an increased eosinophil result.

Eosinophils should be interpreted alongside the total WBC count and other differential results.

Reference ranges vary between laboratories and may depend on the measurement method.

The reference range printed on the patient's laboratory report should be used when determining whether the result is low, normal, or high.

## Safety note

An abnormal eosinophil result does not by itself establish an allergy, parasitic infection, or other diagnosis.

This knowledge base provides general educational information. The system should not diagnose a medical condition based on an eosinophil result alone.
