# Monocytes

## What it is

Monocytes are a type of white blood cell that plays a role in the body's immune system. They help remove damaged cells and microorganisms and participate in immune and inflammatory responses.

## What the test measures

A monocyte result indicates the amount or proportion of monocytes in the blood.

In a blood differential, monocytes may be reported as a percentage of total white blood cells or as an absolute monocyte count.

## Why it may be tested

Monocytes may be measured as part of a CBC with differential to help evaluate immune responses, infections, inflammation, and conditions affecting blood cells.

## When the result is low

A low monocyte count can occur in association with some infections, bone marrow disorders, certain medicines or treatments, and other conditions affecting blood cell production.

A low result alone does not identify the underlying cause.

## When the result is high

A high monocyte count, called monocytosis, can occur with certain infections, inflammatory conditions, and some blood disorders.

A high result alone does not identify the underlying cause.

## Important context

Monocytes may be reported as a percentage or as an absolute count. These measurements should not be treated as interchangeable.

A monocyte percentage can change because the proportions of other white blood cell types change, even when the absolute monocyte count remains within its reference range.

Monocytes should therefore be interpreted alongside the total WBC count and the other differential results.

Reference ranges vary between laboratories and may depend on the measurement method.

The reference range printed on the patient's laboratory report should be used when determining whether the result is low, normal, or high.

## Safety note

An abnormal monocyte result does not by itself establish an infection, inflammatory condition, or other diagnosis.

This knowledge base provides general educational information. The system should not diagnose a medical condition based on a monocyte result alone.
