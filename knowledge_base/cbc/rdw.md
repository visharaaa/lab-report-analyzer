# RDW

## What it is

RDW stands for red cell distribution width. It describes how much the size and volume of red blood cells vary from one another.

## What the test measures

An RDW measurement indicates the degree of variation in the size of red blood cells. It is one of the red blood cell indices included in a complete blood count (CBC).

## Why it may be tested

RDW is commonly used together with other red blood cell indices to help evaluate anemia and other conditions affecting red blood cells.

## When the result is low

A low RDW means that the red blood cells are relatively similar in size.

A low RDW is generally not considered a sign of anemia by itself and usually needs to be interpreted alongside other CBC results.

## When the result is high

A high RDW means that there is greater variation in the size of the red blood cells than expected based on the laboratory's reference range.

A high RDW can occur in several conditions affecting red blood cells and may provide additional information when evaluating different types of anemia.

A high RDW alone does not identify the underlying cause.

## Important context

RDW should be interpreted together with MCV, MCH, MCHC, hemoglobin, hematocrit, and RBC count.

The combination of RDW and other RBC indices can provide more useful information than RDW alone.

Reference ranges can vary between laboratories. The reference range printed on the patient's laboratory report should be used when determining whether the result is low, normal, or high.

## Safety note

An RDW result outside the provided reference range does not by itself establish a diagnosis.

This knowledge base provides general educational information. The system should not diagnose anemia or another medical condition based on RDW alone.
