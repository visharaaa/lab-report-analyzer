# MCV

## What it is

MCV stands for mean corpuscular volume. It describes the average size of a person's red blood cells.

## What the test measures

An MCV test measures the average volume of red blood cells. It is one of the red blood cell indices included in or calculated from a complete blood count (CBC).

## Why it may be tested

MCV is commonly used as part of a CBC to help evaluate red blood cells and to help characterize different types of anemia.

## When the result is low

A low MCV means that the red blood cells are smaller than expected based on the laboratory's reference range.

A low MCV can be associated with conditions such as iron deficiency anemia and thalassemia. Other causes are also possible.

A low MCV alone does not identify the underlying cause.

## When the result is high

A high MCV means that the red blood cells are larger than expected based on the laboratory's reference range.

A high MCV can be associated with certain types of anemia, including anemia related to vitamin B12 or folate deficiency. It can also occur in association with liver disease and other conditions.

A high MCV alone does not identify the underlying cause.

## Important context

MCV should be interpreted together with other red blood cell measurements, including hemoglobin, hematocrit, RBC count, MCH, MCHC, and RDW.

MCV can help describe the size of red blood cells, but it does not by itself determine the cause of an abnormal result.

Reference ranges can vary between laboratories and may depend on factors such as age and other characteristics.

The reference range printed on the patient's laboratory report should be used when determining whether the result is low, normal, or high.

## Safety note

An MCV result outside the provided reference range does not by itself establish a diagnosis.

This knowledge base provides general educational information. The system should not diagnose anemia or another medical condition based on MCV alone.
