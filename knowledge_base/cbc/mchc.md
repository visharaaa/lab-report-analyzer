# MCHC

## What it is

MCHC stands for mean corpuscular hemoglobin concentration. It describes the average concentration of hemoglobin within red blood cells.

## What the test measures

An MCHC measurement indicates how concentrated the hemoglobin is within the red blood cells. It is one of the red blood cell indices reported as part of a complete blood count (CBC).

## Why it may be tested

MCHC is used together with other CBC measurements to evaluate red blood cells and help characterize different types of anemia.

## When the result is low

A low MCHC means that the concentration of hemoglobin within red blood cells is lower than expected based on the laboratory's reference range.

A low MCHC can occur in some types of anemia, including conditions associated with reduced hemoglobin production.

A low MCHC alone does not identify the underlying cause.

## When the result is high

A high MCHC means that the concentration of hemoglobin within red blood cells is higher than expected based on the laboratory's reference range.

A high MCHC can occur in some conditions affecting red blood cells, including certain types of anemia. Laboratory or measurement factors can also affect the result.

A high MCHC alone does not identify the underlying cause.

## Important context

MCHC should be interpreted together with MCV, MCH, RDW, hemoglobin, hematocrit, and RBC count.

MCHC represents the concentration of hemoglobin within red blood cells and is different from MCH, which represents the average amount of hemoglobin per red blood cell.

Reference ranges can vary between laboratories.

The reference range printed on the patient's laboratory report should be used when determining whether the result is low, normal, or high.

## Safety note

An MCHC result outside the provided reference range does not by itself establish a diagnosis.

This knowledge base provides general educational information. The system should not diagnose anemia or another medical condition based on MCHC alone.
