# Platelet Count

## What it is

Platelets, also called thrombocytes, are small blood components that help the blood form clots and stop bleeding.

## What the test measures

A platelet count measures the number of platelets in the blood. It is commonly included as part of a complete blood count (CBC).

## Why it may be tested

A platelet count may be performed as part of a routine CBC or to investigate or monitor conditions that can affect blood clotting or platelet production.

## When the result is low

A low platelet count, called thrombocytopenia, can occur when the body does not produce enough platelets, when platelets are destroyed or used faster than they are produced, or when the spleen holds onto too many platelets.

Possible causes include certain infections, immune conditions, some medicines, and conditions affecting the bone marrow.

A low platelet count alone does not identify the underlying cause.

## When the result is high

A high platelet count, called thrombocytosis, can occur when the body produces too many platelets.

It may occur in response to conditions such as infection, inflammation, blood loss, iron deficiency, or removal or reduced function of the spleen. Some bone marrow disorders can also cause high platelet counts.

A high platelet count alone does not identify the underlying cause.

## Important context

Platelet results should be interpreted together with other CBC findings and relevant clinical information.

The significance of an abnormal platelet count depends on the degree of the abnormality and the underlying cause.

Reference ranges can vary between laboratories. The reference range printed on the patient's laboratory report should be used when determining whether the result is low, normal, or high.

## Safety note

A platelet result outside the provided reference range does not by itself establish a diagnosis.

This knowledge base provides general educational information. The system should not diagnose a bleeding or clotting disorder based on a platelet count alone.
