# Basophils

## What it is

Basophils are a type of white blood cell involved in the body's immune and inflammatory responses.

They can release substances such as histamine and play a role in allergic and inflammatory reactions.

## What the test measures

A basophil result indicates the amount or proportion of basophils in the blood.

In a blood differential, basophils may be reported as a percentage of total white blood cells or as an absolute basophil count.

## Why it may be tested

Basophils may be measured as part of a CBC with differential to evaluate white blood cell patterns and investigate immune, allergic, inflammatory, or blood-related conditions.

## When the result is low

Low basophil levels are often difficult to interpret because basophils normally make up a very small proportion of white blood cells.

Low levels can occur in association with some infections, allergic reactions, certain medicines, or other conditions.

A low result alone does not identify an underlying condition.

## When the result is high

A high basophil count, called basophilia, can occur with some allergic or inflammatory conditions.

Increased basophils can also occur with certain blood disorders and other conditions affecting blood cell production.

A high result alone does not identify the underlying cause.

## Important context

Basophils may be reported as a percentage or as an absolute count. These measurements should not be treated as interchangeable.

Because basophils normally represent a small proportion of white blood cells, small numerical changes can produce noticeable changes in percentages.

Basophils should therefore be interpreted alongside the total WBC count and the other differential results.

Reference ranges vary between laboratories and may depend on the measurement method.

The reference range printed on the patient's laboratory report should be used when determining whether the result is low, normal, or high.

## Safety note

An abnormal basophil result does not by itself establish an allergy, inflammatory condition, blood disorder, or other diagnosis.

This knowledge base provides general educational information. The system should not diagnose a medical condition based on a basophil result alone.
