# Hematocrit

## What it is

Hematocrit is the proportion of the blood that is made up of red blood cells. It is also commonly referred to as packed cell volume (PCV).

## What the test measures

A hematocrit test measures the percentage of blood volume occupied by red blood cells. It is usually included as part of a complete blood count (CBC).

## Why it may be tested

A hematocrit measurement may be used as part of a routine CBC or to help investigate conditions that affect red blood cells, such as anemia or conditions associated with an unusually high number of red blood cells.

## When the result is low

A low hematocrit can be associated with anemia, blood loss, nutritional deficiencies, or conditions that affect red blood cell production.

A low result alone does not identify the underlying cause.

## When the result is high

A high hematocrit can occur when there are more red blood cells than usual or when the blood has less fluid than usual.

Possible causes include dehydration, smoking, living at high altitude, and certain conditions that affect oxygen levels or red blood cell production.

A high result alone does not identify the underlying cause.

## Important context

Hematocrit should be interpreted alongside hemoglobin, RBC count, and other red blood cell indices.

A person's hydration status can affect hematocrit results because changes in the amount of fluid in the blood can change the relative proportion of red blood cells.

Reference ranges can vary between laboratories and may depend on factors such as age, sex, pregnancy, and other characteristics.

The reference range printed on the patient's laboratory report should be used when determining whether the result is low, normal, or high.

## Safety note

A hematocrit result outside the provided reference range does not by itself establish a diagnosis.

This knowledge base provides general educational information. The system should not diagnose anemia or another medical condition based on hematocrit alone.
