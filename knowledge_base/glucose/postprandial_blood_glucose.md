# Postprandial Blood Glucose

## What it is

Postprandial blood glucose refers to the amount of glucose in the blood after eating a meal.

## What the test measures

A postprandial blood glucose test measures blood glucose at a specified time after a meal.

The timing of the measurement is important because glucose levels normally change after food is consumed.

## Why it may be tested

Postprandial blood glucose may be measured to understand how the body responds to a meal or to monitor blood glucose levels in people with diabetes or other glucose-related conditions.

## When the result is low

A low blood glucose level after eating is called hypoglycemia.

Low glucose can occur for several reasons, including certain medicines, changes in food intake, increased physical activity, or other medical conditions.

The significance of a low result depends on the glucose level, timing of the test, symptoms, medications, and individual circumstances.

## When the result is high

A high postprandial blood glucose result means that the blood glucose level is higher than expected based on the laboratory's reference range or the relevant clinical criteria.

Elevated post-meal glucose can occur when the body does not produce enough insulin or does not respond effectively to insulin.

A single elevated result does not by itself establish a diagnosis.

## Important context

The timing of the blood sample after eating is essential when interpreting a postprandial glucose result.

Postprandial glucose should not be interpreted using the same criteria as fasting blood glucose.

The patient's meal, timing of the test, medicines, physical activity, illness, and other factors may influence the result.

The reference range printed on the patient's laboratory report should be used when determining whether the result is low, normal, or high within this application.

## Safety note

A postprandial blood glucose result outside the laboratory's reference range does not by itself establish a diagnosis.

This knowledge base provides general educational information. The system should not diagnose diabetes, hypoglycemia, or another medical condition based on a postprandial glucose result alone.
