# Random Blood Glucose

## What it is

Blood glucose, also called blood sugar, is the main type of sugar found in the blood and provides energy for the body's cells.

## What the test measures

A random blood glucose test measures the amount of glucose in the blood at a particular time, without requiring the person to fast beforehand.

## Why it may be tested

A random blood glucose test may be used when a person needs their blood glucose checked without waiting for a fasting period.

It may also be used when diabetes is suspected, particularly when someone has symptoms associated with high blood glucose.

## When the result is low

A low blood glucose level is called hypoglycemia.

Low glucose can occur for several reasons, including certain medicines, not eating enough, increased physical activity, alcohol use, or some medical conditions.

The significance of a low result depends on the glucose level and the individual's circumstances.

## When the result is high

A high blood glucose level is called hyperglycemia.

High glucose can occur when the body does not produce enough insulin, does not respond effectively to insulin, or because of other factors affecting glucose regulation.

A high random blood glucose result can provide important information when diabetes is suspected, particularly when accompanied by symptoms. However, interpretation depends on the clinical context and the measurement.

## Important context

Unlike fasting blood glucose, random blood glucose does not require fasting.

The result can be affected by factors such as when the person last ate, physical activity, medicines, illness, and other circumstances.

Random blood glucose should not be interpreted using fasting blood glucose or HbA1c thresholds.

The reference range printed on the patient's laboratory report should be used when determining whether the result is low, normal, or high within this application.

## Safety note

A random blood glucose result outside the laboratory's reference range does not by itself establish a diagnosis.

This knowledge base provides general educational information. The system should not diagnose diabetes, hypoglycemia, or another medical condition based on a random blood glucose result alone.
